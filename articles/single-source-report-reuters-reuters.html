Reuters' 21 October IBM report: software revenue growth outlook and the deal-timing test

Reuters reported a higher lower bound for IBM's annual software revenue growth outlook on 21 October 2026. Its account of deferred deals leaves a question about repeatable demand.

Published 05 Nov 2026, 12:56:14 GMT+7 · Capital Chronicle

Reuters reported on 21 October 2026 that IBM's annual software-revenue growth outlook was about 7% to 8%, compared with 6% to 8% previously. The reported revision raised the lower bound and left the upper bound unchanged. How much of the quarter reflected fresh business, and how much depended on transaction timing?

Reuters put third-quarter total revenue at $17.10 billion, with software revenue of $7.80 billion. These are quarterly results as reported by Reuters; the annual growth range is management's expectation. Software revenue is included in IBM's total revenue.

Kavanaugh told Reuters that approximately 25% of transaction-processing revenue reflected deals postponed from the second quarter; he described the other 75% as new business. Both percentages refer to transaction-processing revenue. The split distinguishes delayed deals from new business within that revenue category.

If Reuters' account is accurate, delayed transactions contributed to the quarter alongside new business. That gives two possible explanations for the reported improvement: stronger ongoing demand and a recovery in the timing of deals. The raised lower bound remains management's expectation; the reported quarter alone cannot establish how durable any improvement would be.

The next useful test is whether further reported results substantiate the new-business explanation. More detail on the contribution from deferred transactions would help distinguish deal timing from repeatable demand. The 21 October 2026 report does not establish that subsequent outcome.
