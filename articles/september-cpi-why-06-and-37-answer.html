September CPI: why 0.6% and 3.7% answer different questions

In its October 14 release on September 2026 CPI, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported monthly and annual readings with different horizons and adjustment bases.

Published 03 Nov 2026, 10:49:41 GMT+7 · Capital Chronicle

In September 2026, all-items CPI rose 0.6% and CPI excluding food and energy rose 0.3%, both seasonally adjusted, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics release of October 14, 2026. The difference is clear; its cause and persistence are separate questions. This is a retrospective of that release.

The September snapshot

Over the twelve months ending September, all-items CPI-U increased 3.7%; CPI-U excluding food and energy increased 2.6%. Those annual figures are not seasonally adjusted. They describe a different horizon from the monthly readings.

Two horizons, two adjustment bases

Monthly and twelve-month readings in the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' October 14, 2026 release on September CPI have different horizons and adjustment bases. Comparing 0.6% with 3.7% as competing answers would confuse those distinctions.

Within the monthly pair, the all-items rise was larger than the rise excluding food and energy. That comparison establishes a difference between the measures. It does not, by itself, identify a component contribution or show whether either rate was speeding up or slowing down.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' September CPI release of October 14, 2026 showed a lower monthly reading excluding food and energy. Giving that narrower measure more weight is an analytical choice, not proof that the all-items increase was temporary. Giving the headline more weight does not establish persistence either.

What these figures leave open

Reading the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' October 14, 2026 release on September CPI retrospectively raises a separate question: what did contemporaneous market prices show? A claim about reception would need dated price evidence; a surprise claim would need a dated expectation. The CPI readings alone cannot supply either conclusion.
